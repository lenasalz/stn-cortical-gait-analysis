Results: "The absence of a Cohort x GaitPhase interaction is not explained by low sensitivity. After removing each participant's mean over gait phases, the phase-specific PD - HC differences were small (largest 0.72 dB, theta IDS; all other bands within +/-0.5 dB, beta and gamma within +/-0.3 dB) and statistically equivalent to zero at a 1 dB bound in all four phases for low beta, high beta and gamma and in three of four phases for theta and alpha (Welch TOST, 90 % CIs in Table X). BIC-based Bayes factors favoured the model without the Cohort x GaitPhase term in every band (BF01 198-1218 with the default BIC; 9-54 with the number of participants as effective sample size) and without the Cohort x Area x GaitPhase term (BF01 730-1998; 32-88). Simulations from the fitted random-slope model showed 80 % power to detect a single-phase PD - HC difference of 1.0 dB in the beta and gamma bands and 1.5 dB in theta and alpha (empirical type I error 0.045-0.085), so any undetected phase-specific difference is smaller than about 1 dB (beta, gamma) to 1.5 dB (theta, alpha), i.e. below half of the gamma cohort offset of -2.4 to -2.8 dB. The gamma offset itself received only weak support from the Bayes factor (BF10 = 2.9; BF10 = 1.0 for high beta)."

Caveats to state: the TOST bounds (1.0 and 0.5 dB) were chosen as about 40 % and 20 % of the observed gamma offset, not from an independent effect-size benchmark; the BIC Bayes factors assume a unit-information prior and are driven by the penalty when the likelihood ratio is near zero; power was simulated for an effect confined to one phase (RLS), the configuration the submitted model had suggested, and with the observed cycle counts, so it is the sensitivity of this design, not a general statement. The Cohort x Area offset difference (motor - frontal) is not equivalent to zero at either bound in any band (gamma +1.0 dB, 90 % CI -0.5 to 2.5), so no claim of area-specific equivalence can be made.
